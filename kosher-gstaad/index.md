Gstaad sits at about 1,050 m in the Saanenland, canton of Bern, with Glacier 3000 above it at 3,000 m.

## What we organise *in Gstaad*

- **A chalet or apartment with a private chef.** Our kosher team based in the Alps makes the kitchen kosher and the chef cooks on site, every meal of the week. In winter you come back from the Eggli or the Wispile to a set table; in summer, from the trails or a concert of the Menuhin Festival.
- **The kosher shopping, brought in.** Meat, wine, challot and dry goods travel with the chef, declared at the Swiss border where needed; the rest is bought in the kosher shops of Geneva, Lausanne or Bern on the way. Tell us where you sleep: in Gstaad itself, in Saanen, Schönried or Saanenmöser, or up in a hamlet.
- **Shabbat.** Shabbat meals, challot and wine are cooked and set before candle lighting. Nothing is prepared on Shabbat or on a holiday.
- **The holidays.** Hanukkah, Purim, Pesach or Sukkot in the Bernese Oberland. Tell us which holiday falls in your week.
- **A minyan and a Sefer Torah.** No synagogue is listed in Gstaad or in the Saanenland, so a minyan is built from the families who write to us for the same week. Give us your dates early and we group them, with a Sefer Torah.
- **A tailor-made stay, A to Z.** Chalet, chef, transfers from Geneva or Zürich, Shabbat, the holidays, a minyan: one request, one quote. Families and groups from abroad are our core, including large groups and Hebrew-speaking guests. Gstaad is more than three hours from our kitchen in the French Alps, so we do not deliver meals there: the chef comes to you.

Strictly kosher. Our kosher team based in the Alps cooks under the Chabad network of the Alps. Detail of each service: [kosher services in the Alps](https://mishpachatours.com/kosher-services-french-alps/).

## Kosher *in Gstaad*

Supervision is rarely written down, so check before you book.

- **Kosher restaurant, shop or hotel.** None found in the resort. Plan on your chef, or bring your food.
- **Synagogue and Chabad house.** None is listed in Gstaad or in the Saanenland. The nearest synagogues are in Bern, about 1 h 30 away, and in Lausanne and Geneva on the road from the airport.
- **Nearest large communities.** Geneva, Lausanne and Bern have synagogues, kosher shops and caterers. Plan any shopping there before you drive up.
- **Season.** The Gstaad lifts usually run from early December to early April; Glacier 3000 opens earlier in autumn and closes later in spring. Check the dates for your week before you book.

## Winter and summer *in Gstaad*

- **Winter.** The Gstaad ski area links the Saanenland and the Pays-d'Enhaut, about 200 km of runs between the village slopes and Glacier 3000. The slopes stay gentle and sunny, well suited to families; the glacier adds high snow for the stronger skiers. Sunset comes early on a Friday in December, so Shabbat meals are set well before the lifts close.
- **Summer.** Walking trails from every village, the lakes of Lauenen and Arnensee, and the Menuhin Festival with classical concerts from July to September. Packed lunches for the trail are prepared by the chef with notice.
- **Minyan and Shabbat.** With no synagogue in the resort, every minyan in Gstaad is built from the families who write to us, with a Sefer Torah. Shabbat times appear live on this page.

## Getting *to Gstaad*

- **From Geneva airport:** about 2 h by road along the lake to Aigle, then up through Les Diablerets and the Col du Pillon, or through Bulle and Château-d'Œx.
- **By train:** about 3 h from Geneva, changing at Montreux for the GoldenPass line to Gstaad.
- **From Zürich airport:** about 2 h 45 by road through Bern and Spiez; by train about 3 h with changes at Bern and Zweisimmen.
- **From Bern:** about 1 h 30 by car.
- **From Lyon-Saint-Exupéry:** about 3 h 45 by road through Geneva. Lyon suits long-haul flights and a first kosher shop before you climb: [Lyon, the gateway to the Alps](https://mishpachatours.com/lyon-gateway-to-the-alps-kosher/).
- **Crossing from France:** carry a passport or identity card for every traveller, children included, and buy the Swiss motorway vignette online before the border. The kosher products travel with the chef and are declared at the border where needed.
- **Land in Lyon with a few hours to spare?** [The Stopover](https://mishpachatours.com/tours/stopover/): a private 3 hour tour of Jewish Lyon between the plane and the road.

## Kosher in Gstaad: *questions*

How much does it cost?

It depends on the dates, the number of people and the menu. You receive a written quote before you commit.

Do you deliver kosher meals to Gstaad?

No. Gstaad is more than three hours from our kitchen in the French Alps, so meals are not delivered there. A private chef comes to your chalet with the kosher shopping and cooks on site.

Is there a minyan in Gstaad?

No synagogue is listed in the resort. A minyan depends on how many families are in Gstaad that week. Give us your dates early and we group them, with a Sefer Torah.

Do you work in summer as well as in winter?

Yes. Gstaad is open to walkers and festival guests in summer and to skiers in winter, and the services are the same: private chef, Shabbat, holidays, minyan.

Can you organise a stay for a large group?

Yes. Groups and families from abroad are our core. Send the number of people, the dates and what you need, and you receive one written quote.

Who supervises the food?

Our kosher team based in the Alps cooks under the Chabad network of the Alps. The supervision details come with your quote.
