Where to find a minyan in winter

Two resorts have a Chabad service. Both are run by Rabbi Daniel Belaïch (habadalpes@yahoo.fr).

  • Courchevel 1850. Services during the winter season, at 389 rue des Tovets. The rabbi writes that he helps with kosher needs, wine and challot for Shabbat, and with a Kaddish.
  • Megève. Services in the 'Maison de la Montagne' room. Write before you go: the venue has changed over the years.
  • Alpe d'Huez, Les Deux Alpes, Serre Chevalier, Chamrousse, Val Thorens, Les Menuires. No standing service. Tell us your dates and your resort: we organise the minyan and a Sefer Torah with the Chabad network of the Alps, which groups the families present. Ask early.
  • Les Arcs, Tignes, Val d'Isère, La Plagne, Chamonix. Private operators (below) organise a minyan when enough people come.

The Chabad network of the Alps is headed by the Beth Habad of Grenoble. Kosher meals, a chef in your chalet, Shabbat and the holidays in the resort are a Mishpacha Tours service: see kosher services in the Alps.

Shabbat on the mountain

Candle lighting in the Alps is not the Lyon time, and it changes every week. Ask your Chabad contact for the service times.

  • Plan the week around it. Lifts and skiing are not part of Shabbat for an observant family. Choose a chalet or hotel close to your minyan, so you can walk.
  • Ask the property three questions. Is there a Shabbat key or a door that opens without a card? Are lights and heating on a timer? Can food be kept warm before Shabbat?
  • Cook before it starts. Prepare the Friday meal on Thursday or Friday morning, and carry the food up with you.
  • Know your nearest minyan. Write it down before you leave Lyon: mobile networks drop in the valleys.

For halakhic questions, ask your own rabbi. We give practical points only. See also Shabbat in Lyon.

Kosher food in the resort

There is no kosher shop in the resorts. Meals, though, can come to you: our kosher team in the Alps delivers to a group for the week, or puts a chef in your chalet. Send us the request. Otherwise bring your food from Lyon: the shops are on our page Lyon, the gateway to the Alps. The Courchevel rabbi offers help with wine and challot. The operators above provide meals in their own programs: ask who supervises.

Private chefs advertise 'glatt kosher' cooking in Megève, Chamonix and Courchevel. Their supervision is not stated on their websites, so we do not list them as kosher hotels. Ask for the certificate.

Safe, by design

We cannot promise zero risk. Our advice is simple.

  • Keep your plans to your group, and do not post your hotel or route in real time.
  • Choose your own level of visibility in public: some travellers wear a cap rather than a kippa outside.
  • Travel with a companion at night.
  • Tell us if you have concerns: we adapt. A professional escort is available on request on our tours, priced separately and quoted on demand.

Kosher ski: questions

Which French ski resorts have a kosher minyan?

Courchevel 1850 and Megève have a Chabad service in winter, run by Rabbi Daniel Belaïch. On the Grenoble side, Alpe d'Huez, Les Deux Alpes, Serre Chevalier, Chamrousse and Val Thorens, we organise a minyan on request with the Chabad network of the Alps, which groups the families present.

Can we get kosher food delivered to the resort?

Yes: our kosher team in the Alps delivers meals to your group for the week, or puts a chef in your chalet. Send us your resort and dates for a quote. Otherwise bring your food from Lyon.

Do observant families ski on Shabbat?

No. Lifts and skiing are not part of Shabbat for an observant family. Choose lodging near your minyan and cook before Shabbat. For a ruling, ask your own rabbi.

How do we fit Lyon into a ski week?

Land in Lyon, shop kosher, take our 3 hour Stopover with the Grande Synagogue, then drive two to three hours to the resort.

Do you sell ski packages or transfers?

No ski passes, no transport. Kosher chalets, chefs and delivered meals in the resorts are a Mishpacha Tours service, organised with our team in the Alps: send us the request.
